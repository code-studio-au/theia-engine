## 1. Creative pillars [CONFIRMED]

Theia is a large-scale high-fantasy MMORPG with layered, unreliable historical traditions, ancient lost magical achievement, living mortal civilizations, and a gradually reawakening existential danger. It should evoke the depth of an epic fantasy saga while remaining original.

The setting must be rewarding independent of the main quest. Launch progression aims at level 60 (**working target**), with a classic open-world MMO model: optional principal campaign chapters, racial starting arcs, substantial self-contained regional adventures, dungeons, professions, exploration, alliance war, and PvP. The entire saga should be planned as creator-facing history before detailed quests are produced. A satisfying launch finale should not disclose every cosmic mystery.

**Hard faction boundary:** Covenant and Dominion are two permanently hostile, politically separate playable alliances. A character's alliance membership is permanent. No ordinary cross-alliance citizenship, trade, friendly quests, public diplomacy, military service, or cooperative gameplay. Exceptions are *deeply clandestine* networks whose agents outwardly retain their native alliance affiliations. The rare historical peace negotiation was a failed exceptional attempt, **not** evidence of ordinary diplomacy.

## 2. Primordial cosmology [CONFIRMED foundation; expanded creator draft 2026-10-12]

**Primordial status:** Aethyr and Nhal were supreme primordial essences, categorically greater and more fundamental than the Seven. They existed **before space, time, realms or physical laws**. The later descriptions of their struggle lasting “eons” are figurative: time did not yet exist to measure it.

- **Aethyr** embodied the power and will of existence, creation, possibility and becoming.
- **Nhal** embodied negation and unmaking, the power and will to destroy what Aethyr sought to bring into existence.

Their opposed creative and destructive wills produced an immeasurable **stalemate**. Neither could overcome the other by direct force; while Nhal could reach Aethyr's creations, none could be assured of enduring.

**Aethyr's revelation and objective:** Instead of destroying Nhal, Aethyr conceived a means of **confining Nhal apart from creation**. It would establish two separate realms, place Nhal beyond the realm in which creation could develop, and maintain a boundary preventing unmaking from reaching across. The plan would require Aethyr's deliberate self-destruction.

**The First Sundering:** Aethyr shattered itself by choice. The resulting eruption of primordial energy **created both the Physical Realm and the Void**—neither realm existed before this event. The force drove Nhal into the newly created Void and Aethyr's shattered energies into the Physical Realm. In its final act Aethyr established the **Veil between those realms**, deliberately protecting the Physical Realm from Nhal's unmaking. The Physical Realm thereby acquired space, time, matter and fundamental magic. The Void is a realm of negation, **not** an ordinary physical world with its own preexisting geography. Nhal endured within it because of its primordial nature, but was confined by the Veil.

**The Seven:** Seven independent equal divine consciousnesses subsequently awakened from Aethyr's shards within the Physical Realm. They were not Aethyr reborn as a single mind, did not inherit its full memories or understand the primordial sacrifice, and had no supreme ruler. Magic is part of the physical reality made possible by the First Sundering; the Seven learned its laws rather than inventing it.

**Status distinction:** The user's 2026-10-12 clarification explicitly establishes the *intentional imprisonment plan*, the *simultaneous creation of two realms*, Nhal's *forced displacement*, and the Veil's *deliberate creation as Aethyr's final protective act*. The user expressly calls this telling **a draft, not final prose**; narrative phrasing remains revisable. The Veil's existence and intended protective purpose are now part of the draft's established direction; its technical mechanisms, the precise nature of boundary damage, and how Nhal might eventually cross remain **WORKING/OPEN**, not resolved by this clarification.

## 4. Divine and mortal history

The Seven discovered magical laws and cooperated in creating mortal peoples. Multiple gods could combine power for very rare creative acts; this is a **divine** exception, not a general multi-player spellcasting rule.

The **Divine Age / Age of Wonders** featured extraordinarily sophisticated magical civilizations, several great independent magical orders, broad knowledge sharing, libraries, scholarly exchanges, and ancient Passage networks. Divine influence took the form of founding teachings, rare direct instruction and mortal intermediaries. Mortal scholars could openly challenge divine ideas.

The Seven's schism centred on creation of an extraordinarily powerful **vanished seventh people**. Some gods were directly involved in their creation. Nhal subtly manipulated individuals and evidence, exploiting genuine values and mistrust. The Divine War then drew mortal civilizations into complex, changing conflicts; it **was not** simply modern Covenant versus Dominion.

**The Binding — CONFIRMED creator-facing truth (2026-10-12):** After the catastrophe surrounding the First Anchor and the ensuing divine conflict, the Seven join their divine essences to create and sustain **the Shroud**, containing the breach and the devastated seventh people's homeland. **Keeper knew with certainty before the ritual began that all Seven would become bound to maintaining the Shroud, unable to freely withdraw or resume their former independence. Keeper deliberately concealed this fact.** The others believed they were contributing a limited portion of their essence, not committing themselves to indefinite confinement. Their participation therefore lacked informed consent. The historical event is called **The Binding**; **Keeper's betrayal** is its hidden moral truth, not the event's public title. All seven essences and individual consciousnesses survive within the binding, preserving the possibility of a later **voluntary** reunification against Nhal. Whether that reunification occurs remains OPEN. The precise magical mechanism, dialogue, and Keeper's other alternatives remain subject to further development.

**Supersession:** Earlier proposals in which Guardian and Keeper secretly commandeered mortal infrastructure as the decisive betrayal, or in which some of the Seven died during the sealing, are no longer the governing version. The original source decision that some gods “fell” should be understood in light of this later decision: they may have fallen from active freedom or influence, **not perished and lost their essences**.

The Divine War devastated ancient civilizations, destroyed or transformed many magical structures and passageways, triggered migrations, and reduced surviving magical knowledge. The two primary modern continental heartlands and central war-ravaged **Shrouded Lands** emerge from this history; the precise former single-landmass geography is **WORKING** rather than finally mapped.

Postwar societies preserved partial, conflicting accounts. Distinct ancient ancestry and altered religious patronage explain the eight modern civilizations. Different races preserved different fragments of magical knowledge.

## Anchor taxonomy and player destruction rules [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

**First Anchor (ancient, unique):** Built by the seventh people's greatest researchers, believing it would create a lasting connection with divinity; Nhal's influence redirected its design. Activation established a persistent breach in Aethyr's **Veil**. The original structure became **entangled with the breach itself**. It is **not physically indestructible**: breaking it while the breach remains could remove the only remaining structure restraining uncontrolled expansion, potentially worsening the catastrophe. This is why the Seven contained the site through **The Binding** and the **Shroud** instead of simply destroying the First Anchor. The original site lies in the devastated seventh people's homeland beneath the Shroud.

**Modern Unmade Anchors (derivative, destructible):** The seventh people's complete designs, specialist institutions and practical knowledge were lost or isolated in the Second Sundering. The Unmade preserved fragments, diagrams, artifacts and partial procedures but cannot reliably reproduce the First Anchor. Their modern devices are **imperfect secondary resonators**, exploiting the **pre-existing damage and connection** associated with the First Anchor rather than opening an independent permanent Void breach each time. They can extend Nhal's influence locally, but their connections are limited and dependent on vulnerable physical/magical support structures. **Players can disable or destroy these devices**, sometimes only after disconnecting supports and stabilizing the site. A failed or reckless dismantling can cause a local hazard, but does not automatically repeat the Second Sundering.

**Escalation and gameplay:** (1) **Minor Resonators**: early-game local disturbances and corrupted creatures, typically destructible after defeating defenders; (2) **Regional Anchors**: mid-game installations in ruins, mines, temples or research bases, requiring safe disconnection of supports; (3) **Major Anchors**: rare late-game/dungeon/raid objectives assembled from stronger artifacts and sophisticated reconstructions, requiring staged stabilization and destruction. These are **narrative and progression categories**, not finalized numerical tuning, mandatory zone names or fixed instance counts.

**Strategic effect:** Minor devices create local influence; regional devices extend it; **only sophisticated coordinated networks of major Anchors** can substantially strain The Binding and threaten the Shroud. This supports geographically distributed Guardian/Unmade questlines and independent Covenant/Dominion campaigns on both continents without making every low-level device an extinction event. The Unmade seek to reconstruct enough ancient knowledge to coordinate a network, weaken the Shroud, access the First Anchor and ultimately allow Nhal's emergence. Guardians know why unsafe destruction is dangerous and can teach players to sever links before destroying Anchors. The First Anchor's final resolution and the Seven's possible reunification remain **OPEN endgame decisions**.

**Do not conflate:** First Anchor ≠ modern Anchor; the Veil ≠ the Shroud; local resonator destruction ≠ destroying the original breach; and destroying modern Anchors ≠ automatically destroying or freeing the Seven.

## The ancient Passage gates after The Binding [LOCKED · 2026-10-12]

**Continuous low-capacity operation is confirmed.** Before The Binding, the seventh people's homeland was a major hub in an ancient network of sophisticated **Passage** gates used for scholarship, commerce, travel and the movement of substantial groups and goods. The Binding and the Shroud drastically weakened the gates linked to the contained homeland, **but did not shut down every surviving gate**. A limited number of connections remained **continuously operational at low capacity** throughout the intervening centuries; they did **not** depend on rare alignments or periodically opening magical windows.

**Restrictions:** Surviving routes can support discreet travel by individuals or small groups and the movement of limited artifacts and supplies, but cannot sustain army-scale transfers, large military supply lines or open conquest. The network's useful destinations and throughput are greatly reduced, and its operation may be hazardous even though some routes remain continuously available. Passage transit is **between physical locations**; it is not itself a fresh breach of Aethyr's Veil or a means of transporting Nhal bodily into the Physical Realm. The Shroud still contains the First Anchor.

**Historical consequence:** The Unmade preserved knowledge of some surviving routes and exploited them to move operatives, instructions, artifacts and small consignments between the Shrouded Lands and sites on the Covenant and Dominion continents. This enabled long-term covert infiltration, not Unmade armies. Cells already outside the Shroud could also persist and recruit locally. The Unmade's loss of reliable direct communication with Nhal after The Binding is compatible with the gates' continued **physical transit**: access to a Passage gate does not equal a direct connection to the primordial intelligence.

**Modern consequence:** Awakening magic and renewed research may improve access and eventually enable Covenant and Dominion to **independently restore major gateways** for organized expeditions into the Shrouded Lands. These are distinct from conventional overseas shipborne military incursions, which remain part of the war on both continents. Whether corrupted seventh-people creatures occasionally use the weakened gates is a **working possibility**, not a separately locked decision; their principal stronghold reclamation role remains in the Shrouded Lands. Exact gate counts, routes, security, transit rates and gameplay unlocks remain open.

Nhal remains unable to emerge bodily as a free primordial entity simply because the interior is contaminated.

## 5. Magic [CONFIRMED]

Fundamental magic permeates existence. A practitioner channels it through **intent and learned techniques**, spending a **finite personal/internal reserve** shared across disciplines. Natural aptitude, bloodlines and training affect potential, capacity and efficiency. Recovery via natural regeneration, ambient magic and trained meditation. Running out normally prevents casting safely; overreaching complex techniques risks loss of control and mental overload. Skill and precision matter more than raw reserve.

There are **exactly six provisional discipline names**:
1. **Elements & Energy:** elemental and energetic phenomena.
2. **Matter:** shaping/manipulating physical materials; permanent matter from nothing is a lost ancient art.
3. **Life:** biological, spiritual and natural effects; extraordinary resurrection may be possible under severe constraints (time, consent, body and cost).
4. **Form:** magical patterns, structures, conditions, wards and connections.
5. **Mind:** illusion, emotion and magical perception; no casual unrestricted mind control.
6. **Passage:** spatial transfer, links and travel; extensive long-distance teleportation mostly lost.

Basic effects tend to use one discipline; complex effects combine two; extraordinary ancient arts may combine more. **One individual must master all disciplines used in their working; ordinary cooperative multi-mage spellcasting is not available.** Permanent stable magical creation is particularly difficult/lost. Enchantments can be temporary, rechargeable or rarely self-sustaining; true self-sustaining techniques are largely lost.

Every race can eventually study every discipline. Racial/region/bloodline culture changes teachers, common applications, specialties and heritage affinities, **not** exclusive spell access. Conventional engineering and magic compete in society. Magic is widely available in major cities but less accessible in outlying settlements; medical traditions vary by civilization.

In armies, mages can be supports, integrated personnel and rarely exceptional battlefield powers. Martial elites are viable alongside magical elites. Anti-mage counterplay uses equipment, coordinated tactics and specialist formations. No class system, spell catalogue or combat numerical balance is final.

## 8. Great magical orders and ancient knowledge [CONFIRMED]

The Divine Age had **three or four** great magical orders, distinguished by **philosophy, specialization and purpose**. They were independent but interconnected, drew influences from **several** gods, and their scholars could openly challenge divine teachings. They were mostly cooperative until the Divine War, notwithstanding disputes over ethics, knowledge and political influence.

Great archives, exchanges and magical passageways shared knowledge. Surviving masters initially suppressed **specific dangerous advanced techniques**, not whole disciplines. Destruction of institutions, deaths of practitioners and suppression account for lost knowledge. Postwar survivors founded academies, royal institutions and smaller secret orders. Today's academy histories range from genuine continuity through fragments to later reconstruction. Academic rivalry is prominent.

**Exactly one original great magical order still secretly survives.** Which ancient order it was and its exact surviving knowledge are **OPEN** (earlier assistant guesses about a named Order of Preservation must not be silently locked).

Modern magic knowledge is fragmentary. Rediscovery is promoted by stronger wielders, reawakening sites and recovered teachings. Institutions are divided between progress and catastrophic risk.

## Source-reconciled creator history: required chronology and interpretative boundaries

The canonical causal chain must be written as an **interwoven history**, not as isolated race, god and faction profiles:

1. **Aethyr's desperate intentional First Sundering** creates physical reality and the seven independent gods; Nhal survives in the Void. The Seven initially do not know their primordial origin. Magic predates their consciousness.
2. **The Seven learn, disagree and create**: Guardian–Warrior honour versus sacrifice, Seeker–Shaper discovery versus responsibility, Judge's lawful mediation, Keeper's secrecy and Ascendant's envy evolve through specific shared experiences. Q47 selects **mixed divine morality**, not seven uniformly benevolent gods.
3. **Six mortal species and early cities**: Guardian elves, Judge humans, Shaper dwarves, Warrior orcs, Keeper halflings, Keeper–Ascendant trolls. Keeper's stabilization of Ascendant's unauthorized troll experimentation establishes lasting distrust; the original Q309 specifies **Ascendant sought knowledge Keeper possessed**. No race is inherently corrupted. Original magical bloodlines were deliberately empowered by gods (Q42), are inherited through **direct ancestry** (Q35), and can become exceptional (later rare lineages).
4. **Age of Wonders**: three or four independent yet interconnected magical orders; open scholarly challenge to gods, shared archives, libraries, visiting scholars and ancient Passage systems; several divine teaching channels (Q420 revised to D). The orders remained mostly cooperative until war. Their loss followed destruction, deaths and deliberate suppression of **particular dangerous advanced techniques**, not entire disciplines.
5. **The vanished seventh people**: several gods created an exceptionally magically capable people. **Q40 selected hidden corruption of the creation project by the ancient threat**. The first Nhal contact must therefore plausibly affect the project *during its design or creation*, not only long after its civilization flourishes. Exactly how remains a **WORKING** causal mechanism. The people were not inherently evil; they vanished during Divine War. Their final name and fate remain open.
6. **Divine Schism and war**: Nhal secretly engineered the fracture through gradual, individual manipulation. Moral responsibility still belongs to divine and mortal actors. The Great Betrayal/Second Sundering destroyed ancient infrastructure and led to different gods' fates; **exact betrayer, sealing mechanism, divine sides and outcomes remain unresolved**, however detailed a working narrative may be.
7. **Guardians and the Unmade**: Guardians are confirmed as a completely hidden order **founded during the Divine War**; The Unmade developed from Nhal-serving ancient research and infiltrated institutions on both continents. Their loss of direct contact after the Second Sundering and gradual renewed contact during the awakening are **working narrative elaborations** developed in later conversation, not source-confirmed ancient technical rules. The surviving **one original great magical order** is a third institution whose mission, archive and relationship with Guardians remain open (Q436–444 unanswered).
8. **Migration and eight modern civilizations**: the dwarf mountain/subterranean capital and Korrath highland capital survive from the ancient age; original elven capital was destroyed. Humans rebuild in ancestral plains, orcs in grasslands, halflings move from underground valleys to a surface capital after refuge from orcs, Aelari establish a forest monarchy, Vaelari a mountain-lake arcane government, Vorrath an exiled storm-coast religious society. Humans and dwarves form Covenant through a royal treaty, with the High Ruler from a separate human dynastic branch; orcs and halflings form the Dominion core through protection and partnership, later governed by four-civilization leadership trials.
9. **Modern hostility and sabotage**: Covenant and Dominion are permanent hostile allegiances, no ordinary public cross-faction trade or cooperation. The Unmade **orchestrated the Shattered Delegation**, destroying an exceptional peace attempt and intensifying inherited war. Guardians seek evidence without becoming a third playable alliance.
10. **Level-banded overseas incursions and launch story**: both alliances hold hostile military footholds on the other's continent. Scouts, garrisons, fortified resource hubs, archaeological expeditions, enemy NPC patrols and opposing players make the war tangible before access to Shrouded Lands. Guardian/Unmade investigations traverse these zones independently for each alliance. Shrouded Lands territory has source-confirmed hybrid influence decay, overextension, three neutralization outcomes, dynamic outcome determination and organized siege safeguards; no exact numbers are final. At the working level-60 cap, faction-specific campaigns win independent victories over an Unmade anchor attempt without ending the larger cosmic saga.

### Guardian founders' original understanding [USER CONFIRMED · 2026-10-12]

At the founding of the Guardians during the Divine War, the investigators knew that **an unknown influence was manipulating magical research**, including work associated with the First Anchor. They did **not** know that the influence was **Nhal**, a primordial intelligence, or understand its full cosmological origin. Their mission began with protecting evidence, preventing repetition of dangerous research and investigating the hidden manipulation. The later discovery of Nhal's identity must therefore unfold progressively, not be treated as knowledge every Guardian possessed from the start.

**Still proposed, not confirmed:** That the Guardians' founders were a coalition including seventh-people researchers and scholars of the other six peoples; that some seventh-people researchers were abroad or escaped before The Binding; and that descendants outside the Shroud eventually integrated into other civilizations through intermarriage or interbreeding. Biological compatibility, genealogical concealment and surviving hereditary traits have not yet been established.
