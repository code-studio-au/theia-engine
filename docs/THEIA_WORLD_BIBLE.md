## 1. Creative pillars [CONFIRMED]

Theia is a large-scale high-fantasy MMORPG with layered, unreliable historical traditions, ancient lost magical achievement, living mortal civilizations, and a gradually reawakening existential danger. It should evoke the depth of an epic fantasy saga while remaining original.

The setting must be rewarding independent of the main quest. Launch progression aims at level 60 (**working target**), with a classic open-world MMO model: optional principal campaign chapters, racial starting arcs, substantial self-contained regional adventures, dungeons, professions, exploration, alliance war, and PvP. The entire saga should be planned as creator-facing history before detailed quests are produced. A satisfying launch finale should not disclose every cosmic mystery.

**Hard faction boundary:** Covenant and Dominion are two permanently hostile, politically separate playable alliances. A character's alliance membership is permanent. No ordinary cross-alliance citizenship, trade, friendly quests, public diplomacy, military service, or cooperative gameplay. Exceptions are *deeply clandestine* networks whose agents outwardly retain their native alliance affiliations. The rare historical peace negotiation was a failed exceptional attempt, **not** evidence of ordinary diplomacy.

## 2. Primordial cosmology [CONFIRMED foundations]

Before the physical universe there were two primordial energies/consciousnesses:
- **Aethyr** — primordial potential for existence, creation and becoming.
- **Nhal** — primordial unmaking, negation and stillness; also called **the Unseen** in limited lore.

After a primordial struggle that Aethyr could not win directly, **Aethyr deliberately shattered itself in a final desperate act**. The **First Sundering** brought physical existence into being and generated seven distinct, equal divine consciousnesses: **the Seven**. Nhal was blasted outside physical creation into **the Void**, an absolute absence of ordinary matter, space, time, life and physical reality. Nhal survived there.

The Seven began as equals, without a formal supreme god. They inherited power/aspects from Aethyr but became independent individuals and initially lacked full understanding of their origin. Magic itself is more fundamental than the Seven and is not invented by them.

**WORKING metaphysics, not final:** A boundary often provisionally called *the Veil* separates reality from the Void. Only extraordinary workings affecting fundamental reality can produce contact/resonance; ordinary magic does not weaken it. Nhal progresses through contact, influence, persistent anchors and attempted incarnation. Nhal cannot simply step into the world. The Second Sundering's sealing may have restricted deeper ambient magic while holding Nhal back, explaining aspects of the modern awakening. Exact mechanisms and ultimate solution are still open.

## 3. The Seven Gods [CONFIRMED]

These are **canonical divine titles**; individual personal names proposed in earlier chats were **not approved**.

| Title | Magical focus | Character flaw | Creator and patron connections |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Guardian** | Protection, restoration, sacrifice | Excessive self-sacrifice | Created elves; principal patron of humans and Aelari |
| **The Seeker** | Arcane analysis, knowledge, discovery | Intellectual arrogance | No confirmed playable race directly created; patron of halflings and Vaelari |
| **The Warrior** | Physical empowerment, courage, endurance | Uncompromising honour | Created and is patron of orcs |
| **The Shaper** | Construction, transformation, living creation | Ambition | Created and is patron of dwarves; exceptionally beloved among ancient mortals |
| **The Judge** | Magical binding, equilibrium, truth | Inflexibility | Created humans |
| **The Keeper** | Sealing, containment, preservation | Isolation and secrecy | Created halflings; co-created trolls; patron of Korrath |
| **The Ascendant** | Amplification, adaptation, synthesis | Envy and ambition | Co-created trolls with Keeper; patron of Vorrath |

**Renaming history:** The Ascendant supersedes *The Ambitious*. Never revert the divine title.

Established relationships: Guardian and Warrior were the closest loyal pair, disagreeing over honour versus sacrifice. Seeker and Shaper were early partners in discovery and invention but disagreed about ethical limits. Judge mediated among equals; influenced Warrior, questioned Seeker and opposed unrestricted creation. Guardian sought to protect the distant Keeper; Seeker questioned Keeper's secrecy; Judge respected Keeper but disputed exceptions to shared law. Keeper mistrusted Ascendant's experiments; Guardian and Ascendant were philosophical rivals; Ascendant envied Shaper and had no close divine ally. The Warrior respected Judge and maintained distance from Ascendant.

Keeper and Ascendant co-created trolls. Ascendant secretly introduced unauthorized/forbidden modifications during creation; Keeper had to stabilize the result and distrusted Ascendant thereafter. This **does not** make trolls inherently corrupted or defective.

**OPEN:** Exact divine wartime allegiances; which gods died or survived and in what condition; divine appearances, personal names and final destinies. Some earlier proposed detailed outcomes are not confirmed.

## 4. Divine and mortal history

The Seven discovered magical laws and cooperated in creating mortal peoples. Multiple gods could combine power for very rare creative acts; this is a **divine** exception, not a general multi-player spellcasting rule.

The **Divine Age / Age of Wonders** featured extraordinarily sophisticated magical civilizations, several great independent magical orders, broad knowledge sharing, libraries, scholarly exchanges, and ancient Passage networks. Divine influence took the form of founding teachings, rare direct instruction and mortal intermediaries. Mortal scholars could openly challenge divine ideas.

The Seven's schism centred on creation of an extraordinarily powerful **vanished seventh people**. Some gods were directly involved in their creation. Nhal subtly manipulated individuals and evidence, exploiting genuine values and mistrust. The Divine War then drew mortal civilizations into complex, changing conflicts; it **was not** simply modern Covenant versus Dominion.

The **Great Betrayal** was catastrophic, its public history disputed. The ancient enemy's influence and the sealing of its access are core proposed connective lore, but do not treat every detailed Guardian–Keeper sealing action, motivation, or the vanished people's fate as user-ratified fact.

The Divine War devastated ancient civilizations, destroyed or transformed many magical structures and passageways, triggered migrations, and reduced surviving magical knowledge. The two primary modern continental heartlands and central war-ravaged **Shrouded Lands** emerge from this history; the precise former single-landmass geography is **WORKING** rather than finally mapped.

Postwar societies preserved partial, conflicting accounts. Distinct ancient ancestry and altered religious patronage explain the eight modern civilizations. Different races preserved different fragments of magical knowledge.

## 5. Magic [CONFIRMED]

Fundamental magic permeates existence. A practitioner channels it through **intent and learned techniques**, spending a **finite personal/internal reserve** shared across disciplines. Natural aptitude, bloodlines and training affect potential, capacity and efficiency. Recovery via natural regeneration, ambient magic and trained meditation. Running out normally prevents casting safely; overreaching complex techniques risks loss of control and mental overload. Skill and precision matter more than raw reserve.

There are **exactly six provisional discipline names**:
1. **Elements & Energy:** elemental and energetic phenomena.
2. **Matter:** shaping/manipulating physical materials; permanent matter from nothing is a lost ancient art.
3. **Life:** biological, spiritual and natural effects; extraordinary resurrection may be possible under severe constraints (time, consent, body and cost).
4. **Form:** magical patterns, structures, conditions, wards and connections.
5. **Mind:** illusion, emotion and magical perception; no casual unrestricted mind control.
6. **Passage:** spatial transfer, links and travel; extensive long-distance teleportation mostly lost.

Basic effects tend to use one discipline; complex effects combine two; extraordinary ancient arts may combine more. **One individual must master all disciplines used in their working; ordinary cooperative multi-mage spellcasting is not available.** Permanent stable magical creation is particularly difficult/lost. Enchantments can be temporary, rechargeable or rarely self-sustaining; true self-sustaining techniques are largely lost.

Every race can eventually study every discipline. Racial/region/bloodline culture changes teachers, common applications, specialties and heritage affinities, **not** exclusive spell access. Conventional engineering and magic compete in society. Magic is widely available in major cities but less accessible in outlying settlements; medical traditions vary by civilization.

In armies, mages can be supports, integrated personnel and rarely exceptional battlefield powers. Martial elites are viable alongside magical elites. Anti-mage counterplay uses equipment, coordinated tactics and specialist formations. No class system, spell catalogue or combat numerical balance is final.

## 6. Playable peoples, allegiances and governments [CONFIRMED]

**Covenant** (official name without "The", natural prose "the Covenant"):
- **Humans:** Judge-created, principally Guardian-worshipping; unified monarchy based on ancestral great inland plains. Separate hereditary branch of royal dynasty supplies the **Covenant High Ruler**, whose alliance capital is a great mixed-race coastal metropolis.
- **Dwarves:** Shaper-created and worshipping; confederation of holds; ancient surviving mountain/subterranean racial capital.
- **Aelari:** Guardian-created elves, Guardian-worshipping; woodland monarchy, postwar primeval forest capital.
- **Korrath:** trolls co-created by Keeper + Ascendant; chiefly Keeper-worshipping; elders' council; ancient highland capital surviving Divine War.

**Dominion** (official name without "The", natural prose "the Dominion"):
- **Orcs:** Warrior-created and worshipping; confederation of **Great Clans** (never Great Houses); postwar grassland capital.
- **Halflings:** Keeper-created, principally Seeker-worshipping; guild government, modern surface capital, ancient ancestral underground-valley cities.
- **Vaelari:** Guardian-created elves, chiefly Seeker-worshipping; arcane leadership and postwar mountain-lake capital.
- **Vorrath:** trolls co-created by Keeper + Ascendant; chiefly Ascendant-worshipping; exiled religious civilization led by religious authorities; sacred storm-coast capital.

Dominion's **High Ruler is chosen through trials**, with champions nominated by all four member civilizations. Its huge multicultural alliance capital is in the highlands.

Historical formation: humans and dwarves allied through a royal treaty while maintaining sovereignty; orcs offered halflings refuge and protection, developing a respectful partnership. Aelari and Vaelari split over religion and responsibility in magical research; their original elven capital was destroyed and mutual hostility endures. Korrath and Vorrath split through religious conflict and exile. The specific events and timing of the schisms beyond these foundations are **WORKING**.

Ancestral homelands: human inland plains; dwarf mountains; orc grasslands; halfling underground valleys; elf ancient forests; troll rugged highlands. Postwar migrations spread peoples into varied environments. Each of eight civilizations has one racial capital plus three additional major regional cities; together with the two alliance capitals, the **world-bible target is 34 major urban centres**, **not** a production requirement to build all at launch.

Regional city identities (not finalized names):
- Humans: river city, frontier fortress city, magical/scholarly city.
- Dwarves: volcanic forge, coastal harbour stronghold, monumental surface stone city.
- Orcs: sacred city, military city, coastal city.
- Halflings: engineering city, guild academy city, internal-alliance trade city.
- Aelari: living woodland city, historical scholarly city, coastal city.
- Vaelari: sacred arcane city, frontier expedition city, multicultural trade city.
- Korrath: city of chronicles, ancient ward city, mountain resource city.
- Vorrath: prophecy/divination city, storm harbour city, inland frontier city.

## 7. Cultural martial and magical traditions [CONFIRMED]

Martial arts descend from fragmentary Divine Age knowledge. Fighting style has **strong racial cultural identity**, with local adaptations. Martial orders and magical academies compete for prestige and influence.
- Humans: royal armies, knightly orders, independent martial schools; magic in protection, structured workings and military applications.
- Dwarves: defensive warfare, exceptional engineered gear and siege engineering; Matter, Form and Energy.
- Orcs: Great Clan specialties, professional armies and champions; different clans emphasize different magical arts.
- Halflings: **all three** combat engineering, precision combat and defensive mastery (Q382 changed to D); magic in enchanted mechanisms, logistics/infrastructure and defence.
- Aelari: Life, protective wards, ancestral techniques.
- Vaelari: magical research, perception, advanced combinations; dispute with Aelari centrally about magical responsibility.
- Korrath: ancestral, Keeper-linked highland traditions.
- Vorrath: exile, Ascendant-linked faith and storm-coast adaptations.

Shared martial roots diverged through separate traditions and adaptations during warfare. All racial traditions allow individually diverse fighters and mages.

## 8. Great magical orders and ancient knowledge [CONFIRMED]

The Divine Age had **three or four** great magical orders, distinguished by **philosophy, specialization and purpose**. They were independent but interconnected, drew influences from **several** gods, and their scholars could openly challenge divine teachings. They were mostly cooperative until the Divine War, notwithstanding disputes over ethics, knowledge and political influence.

Great archives, exchanges and magical passageways shared knowledge. Surviving masters initially suppressed **specific dangerous advanced techniques**, not whole disciplines. Destruction of institutions, deaths of practitioners and suppression account for lost knowledge. Postwar survivors founded academies, royal institutions and smaller secret orders. Today's academy histories range from genuine continuity through fragments to later reconstruction. Academic rivalry is prominent.

**Exactly one original great magical order still secretly survives.** Which ancient order it was and its exact surviving knowledge are **OPEN** (earlier assistant guesses about a named Order of Preservation must not be silently locked).

Modern magic knowledge is fragmentary. Rediscovery is promoted by stronger wielders, reawakening sites and recovered teachings. Institutions are divided between progress and catastrophic risk.

## 9. Hidden conflict [CONFIRMED]

**The Guardians:** Founded during the Divine War, completely clandestine and devoted to preserving warnings of the external threat and preventing Nhal's return. Preserve selected forbidden knowledge. Draw deeply embedded operatives from both alliances. Do not become a third public or playable faction.

**The Unmade:** The confirmed name of the second hidden order, dedicated purely to Nhal's return/incarnation. Origins trace to early Nhal-influenced research. Infiltrates courts, military, academies, guilds, temples and other organizations on both continents. Core leadership serves Nhal knowingly; agents may possess different levels of understanding. Their public identities remain Covenant or Dominion.

**Established:** The Unmade orchestrated the attack that destroyed an exceptionally rare attempt at peace between Covenant and Dominion. This event is called the **Shattered Delegation** in the working narrative. Each alliance blamed the other and the war intensified. Normal cross-alliance diplomatic or commercial relations remain absent. The operation's detailed mechanics, victims, location and commanding agent are still **WORKING**.

The Guardians and Unmade may have cross-alliance agents and concealed channels, but the playable political order remains strictly divided. Guardians may covertly assist the player against Unmade threats **without becoming a joinable secondary alliance**.

**New emerging direction from voice discussions:** Guardian and Unmade quest threads can employ the **same incursion infrastructure** as military quests, forming multi-zone, multi-level investigations on either continent. Parallel Guardian cells may advance related goals independently for Covenant and Dominion players. Players never change factions or cooperate openly across alliances.

## 11. Modern war and world access [CONFIRMED direction]

Covenant and Dominion are continually at war, their primary civilizations occupying different continents. No normal peacetime civilian blending. Nonetheless **each alliance maintains hostile overseas military incursions** on the opposing continent: forward scouts, fortified landing garrisons, research sites, resource hubs, mines, supply stations, camps and outposts. The underlying continent remains politically controlled by its native alliance; occupying forces remain enemies.

**Players gain meaningful cross-continental military and clandestine incursion missions during leveling**, encountering hostile guards and opposing players. Geography must distribute incursions across **different level bands**, provide ongoing linked quest threads and create reasons to revisit frontier areas. Incursion quests may connect military war, Guardians, or Unmade investigations in multi-zone arcs.

**WORKING PvP implementation:**
- Starting racial zones are protected; early overseas expeditions start around 10–20 and intensify toward 60.
- Strongly guarded, spawn-safe arrival bases; outside them, contested frontier objectives support open-world PvP and hostile patrols.
- Level-matched frontier pockets, anti-grief/level-disparity protections, alternate mission routes and PvE fallback for objectives.
- Military ship routes and friendly-only expedition transport connect garrisons.
- Time-limited objective control, resource escorts, fortified hubs, scouting, sabotage and player defence.
- Enemy outposts may sit within each continent's territories, **but never count as friendly cross-alliance villages**.

**Working reference zones:** Broken Coast (on Dominion continent with a Covenant beachhead, intended level 24–30) and Ashen Reach (on Covenant continent with a Dominion beachhead, intended level 24–30). Names, maps, routes, counts and rules are not locked.

The **Shrouded Lands** are a separate central war-ravaged region, eventually opened through forgotten ancient magical passageways by epic quest. They become a much larger contested PvP and territorial-war theatre. They should not be trivially available in ordinary launch leveling.

## 12. Full saga and launch story [WORKING]

Broad creator-facing arc:
1. Primordial Aethyr and Nhal; First Sundering.
2. Seven equal gods, mortal races, great magical orders and Age of Wonders.
3. Extraordinary vanished people created; Nhal gains influence through deep magical work.
4. Disputes fracture the Seven; Divine War devastates ancient world.
5. Great Betrayal / Second Sundering seals Nhal back out at catastrophic cost.
6. Mortal migrations, rise of today's racial cultures and Covenant/Dominion.
7. Current war, Shattered Delegation sabotage, magical awakening, Guardians versus Unmade.
8. Rediscovery of ancient passages and wider historical truth in later saga chapters.

The previous suggested name **Aethren** for the vanished people should be retired because it is easily confused with **Aethyr**. Final name, society and fate: **OPEN**. The original names floated for individual gods are also **OPEN**.

The launch aims for an open-world progression to a **working cap of 60**, with six *broad thematic movements* rather than mandatory linear quest gating:
- 1–10: eight racial beginnings.
- 10–20: neighbouring regions, exploration and early dungeons.
- 20–30: alliance war, overseas forays, political conflict.
- 30–40: magic awakening and ancient research.
- 40–50: secret cells and Unmade revelations.
- 50–60: faction-specific operations thwart significant Unmade attempt at a Void anchor.

Covenant and Dominion have **fully separate campaigns** and faction-respecting NPCs/quest routes. By 60, players can know the Unmade is responsible for the Shattered Delegation and that received Divine War history is incomplete; Aethyr/Nhal's full cosmic identities need not be disclosed. Each alliance wins a meaningful defensive victory **without assisting the other**.

**WORKING campaign names and locations:** *Oaths in Ashes*, *The Price of Dominion*, *The First Reckoning*, *The Severed Chain*, Royal Marches, Deepward Holds, Greenward, First Highlands, Great Grasslands, Guilded Valleys, Lakeward Spires, Stormward Coast. The 38-zone content matrix from v8.6 was exploratory, **not** an approved launch development scope.
